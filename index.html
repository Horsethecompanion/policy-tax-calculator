Monetary Policy "Tax" Calculator

How the OCR mechanism redistributes the cost of essential living

In Aotearoa's modern economy, wealth is increasingly held in assets rather than productivity. When the Reserve Bank changes the Official Cash Rate (OCR), it doesn't create inflation — but in an economy structured around asset ownership, its effects are highly regressive. This calculator shows roughly what the OCR "tax" — the increase in essential living costs — amounts to for different income bands, based on actual Stats NZ household spending data. Note: Official inflation (CPI) excludes mortgage costs entirely, even though mortgage payments are directly determined by the OCR. This means the true impact of OCR changes on households is systematically undercounted in official statistics.

Your Position in the Curve

Q2 households are actually the most exposed by spending patterns (69.1% essentials, 30.7% housing). Q3 (moderate $55k-$100k) follows closely at 63.2%. The "squeezed middle" is real — but the peak burden starts lower than $55k, likely because Q2 captures first-home buyers at their most stretched: small deposits, short fixed mortgages, support phasing out. The CPI blind spot is central here: mortgage costs are directly downstream of OCR, yet excluded from official inflation. For the 60% of NZ households who own, the true OCR tax is never fully measured.

Why This Matters — The CPI Gap

  • CPI excludes mortgage costs — only rent is included
  • 60% of NZ households own; mortgage payments are directly tied to OCR
  • For owners, the OCR "tax" = mortgage costs + imputed rent changes
  • For renters, the OCR "tax" = rent increases (lagged) + imputed rent changes
  • Neither fully shows up in official inflation figures
